Guide · Sunday evening

How to calm the Sunday scaries

The Sunday scaries ease when you stop trying to outrun Monday and name it instead. Let every unfinished thing surface, tag each one with a single word, set the weight down on purpose, and let tonight still be tonight. The practice below does that in seven minutes.

What is actually happening

It is Sunday evening and the week has not started. Your mind starts it anyway. Did I reply to that email. The thing I keep forgetting and then remembering. The feeling lands somewhere specific, often the chest or the shoulders, and it has a shape: a list cycling through the same few items, none of them finished.

Nothing is wrong with you. Thinking is what minds do. The trouble is only that the list has nowhere to go, so it keeps circling, and the body treats each lap as if Monday had already arrived.

What helps, in order

  1. Notice where it sits. Chest, shoulders, jaw. Finding it in the body is the first step out of the loop in the head.
  2. Let the whole list surface. Every task, worry, obligation and half-formed plan. The big things and the small ones. You are emptying the mind onto the table so it can stop cycling.
  3. Tag each one with a single word. Planning. Worrying. Replying. Then let it pass. The word is enough; you are not solving anything tonight.
  4. Give it its own time. All of those things are real and deserve attention. That attention has a time, and the time is not now.
  5. Come back to the breath. If something pulls you back in: acknowledge, release, return. The breath is always here and it is patient.

Written down, this looks like a lot. Spoken slowly, with music underneath, it is one soft movement from a crowded mind to a quiet one.

Questions people ask

What are the Sunday scaries?

The dread that arrives on Sunday evening, usually in the chest or shoulders, as the mind starts rehearsing Monday before Monday has happened. It is anticipation, not the week itself.

Why does it get worse in the evening?

As the weekend winds down there is less to occupy attention, so the unfinished list surfaces and the body responds as if the week had begun. Naming each item and setting it down tends to quiet the cycling.

How long does a Sunday evening practice need to be?

Seven minutes is enough to name what is waiting, feel where it sits in the body, and set it down. The practice on this page runs 7:20 and is made to be listened to lying down or sitting, before bed.
