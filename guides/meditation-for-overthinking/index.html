Meditation for overthinking

A meditation for overthinking cannot out-think the thinking. What it can do is move attention somewhere the thoughts cannot follow: the surface beneath you, the rhythm of the breath, the heartbeat. See if you can notice it, close to your ear or somewhere in the body. If you cannot find it, stay with the breath. Let the rhythm be whatever it is. The practice below is nine quiet minutes of that.

What overthinking is doing

Overthinking is the mind trying to finish something that cannot be finished by thinking: a decision with no new information, a conversation that already happened, a future that has not. It feels like work, which is why it is hard to stop. It is not work. It is a loop.

You will not win an argument with a loop. You can stop feeding it. Attention on the body, on something with its own rhythm, gives the loop less to run on. Thinking is what minds do, and it keeps doing it quietly in the background while you listen for something steadier.

What helps, in order

  1. Be still. Find a place where you can be still for a few minutes. Notice where your body meets the surface beneath you.
  2. Listen for the heartbeat. Maybe you hear it close to your ear. Maybe you feel it somewhere in your body. If you cannot find it, that is fine; stay with the breath.
  3. Let the rhythm be whatever it is. No slowing it, no deepening it. Notice what happens without asking anything to change.
  4. Let the music hold the quiet. For now there is nothing more you need to do.
  5. If the thinking is a list, use the second practice below instead. It lets the list surface, tags each item with a word, and sets it down.

Still Enough to Listen is for the loop with no content, the hum of thinking for its own sake. Before the Week Starts is for the loop with content: tasks, worries, the thing you keep forgetting. It was written for Sunday evening and works any evening the list is running.

When overthinking keeps you awake

At night the same loop is a sleep problem, and the answer is a longer descent. Meditation for racing thoughts has the practice for that, and points to the Deep Sleep program when it is most nights.

Questions people ask

Can meditation stop overthinking?

It cannot stop the thoughts and does not try to. It moves attention to something with its own rhythm, the breath or the heartbeat, so the loop has less to run on. With repetition the loop gets quieter and shorter.

What is the best meditation for overthinking?

One that asks nothing of the thoughts. Still Enough to Listen, on this page, has fourteen lines of guidance in nine minutes; the rest is room. If the overthinking is a list of tasks, Before the Week Starts gives the list somewhere to go.

How long should I meditate for overthinking?

Nine minutes is the practice here, and it is enough to change the state. Once a day for a week is a fair trial, ideally at the same time, because the body learns faster with a routine.
