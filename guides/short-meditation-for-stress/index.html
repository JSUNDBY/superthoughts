Short meditation for stress

A short meditation for stress has one job: get the body to stand down before the mind has finished arguing. The three practices below are all free and under ten minutes. Five minutes to let the jaw, shoulders and hands go off duty. Six minutes for the first moments of the morning, before the phone. Nine quiet minutes with your heartbeat. Pick by the moment you are in, not by the length.

Why short works

Stress is a body state before it is a thought. The jaw sets, the shoulders rise, the breath goes shallow, and the mind starts narrating. A long practice is lovely when you have the time. In the middle of a day you do not, and a five-minute practice you actually use beats a thirty-minute one you keep meaning to.

Each of these is built around a real moment and ends with a clear way back into the day. None of them need a cushion, a quiet room or any experience.

Three moments, three practices

  1. After something tense. Five minutes. Teeth apart, shoulders down, hands open, feet on the ground. Made for the minutes right after a hard conversation, and it works for any stress that has you clenched.
  2. First thing in the morning. Six minutes. Before you reach for the phone. The warmth under the covers, an ember in the chest, and a day you get to arrive at first.
  3. When you need to be still. Nine minutes. Find a place to be still, notice where your body meets the surface beneath you, and see if you can hear your heartbeat. Nothing more to do.

Questions people ask

How short can a meditation for stress be and still work?

Three to five minutes is enough to change the body's state if the practice is physical: jaw, shoulders, hands, feet, breath. The shortest practice on this page is five minutes and twenty-five seconds.

What is the best time of day for a short stress meditation?

The moment the stress is happening. The three practices here are built for three different moments: right after something tense, first thing in the morning, and whenever you need to be still.

Do I need headphones or a quiet room?

No. Headphones help with the music, and a closed door helps with everything, but each practice works in a car, at a desk or in bed.
