How to calm down after an argument

The fastest way to calm down after an argument is through the body, not the replay. The conversation is over, but some of it is still in your jaw, your shoulders and your hands. Let your teeth come apart, let the shoulders lower, open the hands, and feel your feet on the floor. The five-minute practice below walks you through it.

Why you still feel it

An argument puts the body on alert, and the body does not stand down the moment the talking stops. The jaw stays set. The shoulders stay up near the ears. The hands close. Meanwhile the mind runs the conversation again, finding the better line you could have said, and every replay tells the body the argument is still happening.

You do not have to sort any of it out right now. You do not need the last word to know what you meant. What helps first is giving the body permission to go off duty.

What helps, in order

  1. Find support. Sit or lie where your whole body is held. Let your eyes close or soften toward the floor. One deep breath in, all the way out.
  2. Start with the jaw. This is where so much of the day gets held. Let the teeth come apart, a small space between them, the tongue resting low and soft. This is how a jaw feels when it is off duty.
  3. Let the ease move down. Shoulders lower, away from the ears. Hands open, fingers heavy and warm.
  4. When the replay starts, come back. You do not need to stop the thoughts. Notice them, and return to the jaw. Teeth apart. Shoulders down. Hands open.
  5. Feel your feet. Whatever was said, you are still here. The ground is still under you.

That sequence takes about five minutes with the voice guiding it. It is a reset, not a resolution. The conversation can be picked up later, by a calmer version of you.

Questions people ask

How long does it take to calm down after an argument?

The body usually settles within a few minutes once you stop replaying the conversation and give it a physical cue to stand down: jaw loose, shoulders lowered, hands open, feet on the ground. The practice on this page takes five minutes.

Why do I keep replaying the argument in my head?

The mind keeps searching for the better line because the body is still on alert, and every replay tells the body the argument is still happening. Settling the body first tends to quiet the replay.

Should I go back and finish the conversation right away?

Not while the jaw is set and the shoulders are up. Let the body settle first. You do not need the last word to know what you meant, and a calmer version of you can pick it up later.
