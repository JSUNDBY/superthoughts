This isn't just a feeling. The science is clear.

Four findings from the research on mindfulness practice, with the source named for each.

  • Harvard Gazette

    Regular meditation practice leads to measurable changes in brain regions associated with memory, sense of self, and empathy.

    Research from Harvard-affiliated scientists at Massachusetts General Hospital found that eight weeks of meditation practice produced measurable changes in brain structure. MRI scans showed increased grey-matter density in the hippocampus, which handles learning and memory, and decreased density in the amygdala, which handles stress and anxiety.

  • JAMA Internal Medicine, 47 trials reviewed

    Mindfulness meditation programs show moderate evidence of improving anxiety, depression, and pain.

    A systematic review of 47 clinical trials found consistent evidence that mindfulness meditation programs reduce anxiety, depression, and pain. The improvements were comparable to those seen with antidepressant medication, without the side effects.

  • Psychoneuroendocrinology

    Brief daily mindfulness practice lowers cortisol and reduces the biological markers of chronic stress.

    Published research demonstrated that consistent mindfulness practice significantly reduces cortisol, the body's primary stress hormone. Participants showed lower baseline cortisol and improved stress recovery, indicating real physiological change from regular practice.

  • Nature, neuroscience reviews

    More mindfulness practice leads to more behavioral flexibility: fewer compulsive patterns and automatic reactions.

    Research published in Nature found that consistent mindfulness practice increases the brain's capacity for behavioral flexibility. That means a greater ability to pause, choose, and respond rather than react. It shows up across eating, sleep, anxiety, and daily decisions.
